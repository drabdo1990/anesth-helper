# Perioperative Anesthesia Reference Parameters & Clinical Benchmarks

> **Source Material:** Benchmarked from standard clinical anesthesia reference texts (*Miller's Anesthesia*, *Oxford Handbook of Anaesthesia*, *Clinical Cases in Anesthesia*).

---

## 1. Biometric Scaling Rules & Obesity Adjustments

### 1.1 Weight Scalar Principles
* **Lean Body Weight (LBW):** Used for lipophilic and hydrophilic hybrid agents to avoid severe overdose in patients with elevated BMI[cite: 8]. Males max 100 kg, females max 70 kg[cite: 8].
  * Drugs scaled to LBW: Propofol induction[cite: 8], Thiopental[cite: 8], Fentanyl/Alfentanil[cite: 8], Remifentanil[cite: 14], Sufentanil[cite: 14], Morphine[cite: 8], Non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockers (Vecuronium, Rocuronium, Atracurium, Cisatracurium)[cite: 8, 14], Paracetamol[cite: 8], Local Anesthetics[cite: 8].
  * Alternatively, NMBDs in obese patients may be dosed on 20% more than lean body mass[cite: 2].
* **Total Body Weight (TBW):**
  * Suxamethonium (Succinylcholine): Dosed on TBW due to increased plasma cholinesterase activity and extracellular fluid volume (maximum 200 mg)[cite: 8].
  * Neostigmine: Dosed on TBW[cite: 8].
  * Sugammadex: Dosed on TBW to avoid recurarization.
* **Ideal Body Weight (IBW) / Predicted Body Weight (PBW):**
  * Mandatory scalar for mechanical ventilation tidal volume ($V_T$) calculations[cite: 1, 8].
  * Systemic lidocaine infusions[cite: 5, 8].

## 2. Perioperative Pharmacopeia & Dosing Parameters

### 2.1 Premedication & Sedation
* **Midazolam:**
  * Premedication (Adult): 2–4 mg PO 1–2 h preop; 1.5–2.5 mg IV/IM[cite: 8]; or 2.5–5 mg (0.03–0.07 mg/kg IM)[cite: 4].
  * Premedication (Pediatric): 0.1 mg/kg IV (max 4 mg)[cite: 8].
  * Infusion Rate: 3–5 mg/h (IV sedation) or 1–4 mg/h (volatile sedation)[cite: 1].
* **Clonidine:**
  * Premedication: 0.5 mg (5 mcg/kg PO)[cite: 4].
  * Regional/Neuraxial Adjunct: 1–1.5 mcg/kg[cite: 7].
* **Lorazepam:**
  * Premedication: 1–4 mg PO; 1.5–2.5 mg IV/IM[cite: 8]; or 2–4 mg (0.03–0.06 mg/kg PO/IM)[cite: 4].
* **Morphine (Premedication):** 5–15 mg (0.2 mg/kg IM)[cite: 4].
* **Meperidine (Premedication):** 50–100 mg (1.0–1.5 mg/kg IM)[cite: 4].
* **Diazepam (Premedication):** 5–10 mg (0.1–0.15 mg/kg PO/IM)[cite: 4].
* **Scopolamine:** 0.3–0.6 mg (0.006 mg/kg IM)[cite: 4].
* **Atropine (Pediatric IV Premed/Vagolysis):** ~10–20 mcg/kg (e.g., 35 mcg at term up to 380 mcg at 12 yr)[cite: 8] or 0.02 mg/kg[cite: 12].

---

### 2.2 Induction & Hypnotic Agents
* **Propofol:**
  * Adult Induction: 1.5–2.5 mg/kg (scaled to LBW)[cite: 8].
  * Pediatric Induction: 2.5–3.5 mg/kg TBW.
  * Target Blood Concentrations:
    * Skin Incision: 2–6 mcg/mL[cite: 2].
    * Minor Surgery: 2.5–7.5 mcg/mL[cite: 2].
    * Major Surgery: 2–6 mcg/mL[cite: 2].
    * Awakening: 0.8–1.8 mcg/mL[cite: 2].
    * Sedation: 1.0–3.0 mcg/mL[cite: 2].
  * Maintenance Infusion: 43–58 mcg/kg/min[cite: 1] or continuous nomogram rates (50–200 mcg/kg/min)[cite: 2].
* **Ketamine:**
  * Adult Induction: IV 1–2 mg/kg; IM 5–10 mg/kg[cite: 8].
  * Pediatric Induction: IV 0.5–2 mg/kg; IM 5–10 mg/kg[cite: 8].
  * General Anesthesia Infusion: 1–3 mg/kg/h (dilution 500 mg/50 mL; initial rate ~10 mL/h for 60 kg adult)[cite: 8].
  * Analgesic Infusion: 0.2–0.25 mg/kg/h (dilution 200 mg/50 mL; initial rate ~3 mL/h for 60 kg adult)[cite: 8].
  * Trauma Mixture Infusion: 0.5 mL/kg/h (200 mg ketamine + 10 mg midazolam + 10 mg vecuronium in 50 mL 0.9% NaCl)[cite: 8].
* **Thiopental (Thiopentone):**
  * Target Plasma Concentration: 7.5–12.5 mcg/mL (with N2O); 35–45 mcg/mL (without N2O)[cite: 2].
  * Scaled to LBW in adults[cite: 8].
* **Methohexital:** Target plasma concentration 5–10 mcg/mL (skin incision/major surgery); 1–3 mcg/mL (awakening)[cite: 2].

---

### 2.3 Opioid Plasma Targets & Infusions
* **Fentanyl:**
  * Target Plasma Concentrations: Incision 3–6 ng/mL; Major surgery 2–5 ng/mL; Awakening < 1–2 ng/mL; Analgesia 1–2 ng/mL[cite: 2].
  * Maintenance Infusion: 100 mcg/h fixed ICU sedation rate[cite: 1]; or continuous nomogram titration (0.9–3.0 mcg/kg/h)[cite: 2].
* **Alfentanil:**
  * Target Plasma Concentrations: Incision 200–300 ng/mL; Awakening < 200–250 ng/mL; Analgesia 50–100 ng/mL[cite: 2].
  * Infusion Nomogram: 0.25–1.5 mcg/kg/min[cite: 2].
* **Sufentanil:**
  * Target Plasma Concentrations: Incision 1–3 ng/mL; Awakening < 0.2 ng/mL; Analgesia 0.02–0.2 ng/mL[cite: 2].
  * Infusion Nomogram: 0.15–1.0 mcg/kg/h[cite: 2].
* **Remifentanil:** Target plasma concentration 4–8 ng/mL (incision/minor surgery); 2–4 ng/mL (major surgery); < 1–3 ng/mL (awakening)[cite: 2].

### 2.5 Local Anesthetics & Admixture Rules

#### Maximum Single-Dose Ceilings
* **Lidocaine:**
  * Plain: 3 mg/kg (Max: 200 mg)[cite: 8, 9] (or 4.5–5.0 mg/kg[cite: 6, 7, 10]; pediatric plain: 5 mg/kg[cite: 7, 10]).
  * With Epinephrine (1:200,000): 7 mg/kg (Max: 500 mg)[cite: 6, 7, 8, 9, 10].
  * Onset: Fast (5–15 min)[cite: 2, 7, 8]; Duration: 30–90 min (plain) up to 120–240 min (with epi)[cite: 2, 8].
* **Bupivacaine:**
  * Plain: 2 mg/kg[cite: 8] (or 2.5 mg/kg without epi[cite: 10]; max 175–225 mg)[cite: 2].
  * With Epinephrine: 2.5–3.0 mg/kg (Max: 225 mg)[cite: 2, 7, 10].
  * Neonates: Reduce maximum dose by 50%[cite: 10].
  * Continuous Epidural Infusion: 0.2–0.4 mg/kg/h[cite: 10].
* **Levobupivacaine:** Max 2 mg/kg (IBW)[cite: 8] (or 2.5 mg/kg, pediatric plain 3 mg/kg, with epi 4 mg/kg)[cite: 7, 10].
* **Ropivacaine:** Max 3 mg/kg (Max: 225–250 mg)[cite: 2, 7, 8, 10].
* **Prilocaine:** Plain: 6 mg/kg[cite: 8] (or 8 mg/kg with epinephrine, max 600 mg)[cite: 2, 8]. Treat methemoglobinemia (> 600 mg) with methylene blue 1–2 mg/kg[cite: 8].
* **Chloroprocaine:** 8–11 mg/kg plain[cite: 6, 10]; 10–14 mg/kg with epinephrine (Max 900 mg)[cite: 2, 6, 10].

#### Systemic IV Lidocaine Infusion Protocol
* **Antiarrhythmic:** 0.5–1 mg/kg bolus, then 10–50 mcg/kg/min (or 1–4 mg/min in adults)[cite: 8, 9].
* **Perioperative Systemic Analgesia:**
  * Loading Bolus: 1.0–1.5 mg/kg IBW over 2–4 min (max over 20 min)[cite: 5, 8].
  * Continuous Infusion: 0.5–2 mg/kg/h on IBW (usual starting rate: 1 mg/kg/h; or 1.5–6 mL/h of 2% neat solution)[cite: 5, 8].

#### Epinephrine Admixture Rule of Thumb
* Diluting 1:1,000 Epinephrine ($1\text{ mg/mL}$):
  * $0.1\text{ mL}$ in $10\text{ mL}$ local anesthetic = **1:100,000** ($10\text{ mcg/mL}$)[cite: 6].
  * $0.1\text{ mL}$ in $20\text{ mL}$ local anesthetic = **1:200,000** ($5\text{ mcg/mL}$)[cite: 6].
  * $0.1\text{ mL}$ in $30\text{ mL}$ local anesthetic = **1:300,000** ($3.33\text{ mcg/mL}$)[cite: 6].

#### LAST Rescue Protocol
* **Intralipid 20%:** Initial bolus of $1.5\text{ mL/kg}$ IV over 2–3 min, followed by continuous infusion at $15\text{ mL/kg/h}$ ($0.25\text{ mL/kg/min}$)[cite: 8].

### 2.6 Vasopressors & Inotropes
* **Norepinephrine:** Infusion range 0.02–0.5 mcg/kg/min (clinical median 0.07–0.11 mcg/kg/min)[cite: 1].
* **Phenylephrine:**
  * Bolus: 20–100 mcg increments (dilute 10 mg in 500 mL 0.9% NaCl = 20 mcg/mL)[cite: 8].
  * Infusion: 30–60 mcg/min (dilute 5 mg in 50 mL 0.9% NaCl = 100 mcg/mL, run at 0–30 mL/h)[cite: 8].
  * Pediatric: 2–10 mcg/kg bolus, then 0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min infusion[cite: 8].
* **Metaraminol:**
  * Bolus: 0.5–2 mg increments (dilute 10 mg in 20 mL 0.9% NaCl = 0.5 mg/mL, give 0.5–1 mL aliquots)[cite: 8].
  * Pediatric: 10 mcg/kg bolus, then 0.1–1 mcg/kg/min[cite: 8].
* **Dobutamine:** Infusion rate 7.0–8.5 mcg/kg/min[cite: 1].
* **Postinduction Hypotension Diagnostic Threshold:** Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) < 65 mmHg (or < 70 mmHg in known chronic hypertension)[cite: 1].

---

### 2.7 Multimodal Analgesic & Emergency Adjuncts
* **Acetaminophen (Paracetamol):**
  * Adult: 1 g IV q6h (max 4 g/24 h)[cite: 14]. Dose on LBW in obesity[cite: 8].
  * Pediatric: 15 mg/kg IV[cite: 8].
* **Ketorolac:** 15–30 mg IV[cite: 6, 14]. Contraindicated in post-Roux-en-Y gastric bypass due to GI ulceration risk[cite: 14].
* **Labetalol:** 5 mg increments IV up to 100 mg; infusion 20–160 mg/h[cite: 8]. Pediatric: 0.2 mg/kg boluses up to 0.5 mg/kg (max 20 mg)[cite: 8].
* **Magnesium Sulfate:**
  * Eclampsia: 4 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g/h for 24 h[cite: 8].
  * Arrhythmias/Severe Bronchospasm: 2 g (8 mmol) over 10 min[cite: 8].
* **Mannitol (20% solution):** 0.25–2 g/kg (typically 0.5 g/kg of 20% solution)[cite: 8].

## 3. Airway Sizing & Equipment Benchmarks

### 3.1 Pediatric Endotracheal Tubes (ETT) Formulas & Selection

#### Formulas (Children $\ge 2$ years)
* **Uncuffed ETT Internal Diameter (mm):**
  $$\text{ID (mm)} = \frac{\text{Age (years)} + 16}{4} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{\text{Age (years)}}{4} + 4.0$$[cite: 3, 7, 8]
* **Cuffed ETT Internal Diameter (mm):**
  $$\text{ID (mm)} = \frac{\text{Age (years)}}{4} + 3.5 \quad (\text{Select 0.5 mm ID smaller than uncuffed})$$[cite: 12]
* **Tube Length / Insertion Depth (cm to lips):**
  * Oral: $\text{Depth (cm)} = \frac{\text{Age (years)}}{2} + 12$ (or $\text{ETT ID} \times 3$)[cite: 8]
  * Nasal: $\text{Depth (cm)} = \frac{\text{Age (years)}}{2} + 15$[cite: 8]

### 3.3 Double-Lumen Endobronchial Tubes (DLT)
* Selection is primarily determined by **patient height** rather than weight (airways correlate significantly better with height, and males have slightly larger airways than females of identical height)[cite: 4]:
  * **Short ($< 165\text{ cm}$ / 4'6" to 5'5"):** 35 to 37 French left-sided DLT[cite: 4].
  * **Medium ($165\text{--}178\text{ cm}$ / 5'5" to 5'10"):** 37 to 39 French left-sided DLT[cite: 4].
  * **Tall ($> 178\text{ cm}$ / 5'11" to 6'4"):** 39 to 41 French left-sided DLT[cite: 4].
  * *Clinical Pearl:* Chronic smokers and patients with cystic fibrosis/chronic infections tolerate larger DLTs than predicted by height alone[cite: 4].

---

### 3.4 Pharyngeal Airways & Facemasks
* **Guedel Oropharyngeal Airways:**
  * Sizing Landmark: Distance from the maxillary incisors to the angle of the jaw[cite: 8].
  * Sizes: 000 (very small premature neonate) to 0 (50 mm), 1 (60 mm), 2 (70 mm) in children; 3 (80 mm), 4 (90 mm), 5 (100 mm) in adults[cite: 3, 7, 8].
  * Caution: Do not invert the airway during insertion in infants to avoid palatal damage[cite: 8]; avoid in prone cases with extreme neck flexion to prevent tongue edema[cite: 7].
* **Facemasks:** Newborn, infant, child, and adult sizes 3 (small), 4 (medium), and 5 (large)[cite: 7].

## 4. Mechanical Ventilation & Respiratory Mechanics Benchmarks

### 4.1 Intraoperative Protective Ventilation Baseline
* **Tidal Volume ($V_T$):** **6–8 mL/kg of Ideal Body Weight (IBW)**[cite: 8] (or 5–8 mL/kg[cite: 9]; experimental abdominal benchmark: 7 mL/kg PBW)[cite: 1].
  * *Critical Rule for Morbid Obesity:* Never base $V_T$ on actual weight (TBW); base strictly on height/IBW (typically ~500 mL for short adults, ~700 mL for tall adults)[cite: 3].
* **Respiratory Rate (RR):** 12–16 breaths/min (adjusted to maintain normocapnia)[cite: 9].
* **End-Tidal $CO_2$ ($ETCO_2$) Target:** 35–40 mmHg (or 4.5–5.0 kPa)[cite: 1, 8].
* **PEEP:** Standard baseline $\ge 5\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$ (range 5–10 cmH2O; up to 12 cmH2O in high-PEEP open abdominal trials)[cite: 1, 8].
* **Inspiratory Parameters:** Inspiratory flow 60 L/min; inspiratory pause 0.3 s[cite: 1].

---

### 4.2 Airway Pressure Thresholds
* **Plateau Pressure ($P_{plat}$):** Limit strictly to **$< 30\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$** to prevent barotrauma and alveolar overdistension[cite: 8, 9, 11].
* **Peak Airway Pressure ($P_{aw}$):** Keep $\le 35\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$, ideally $\le 30\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$[cite: 8].
* **Driving Pressure ($\Delta P = P_{plat} - \text{PEEP}$):** Target $< 14\text{--}15\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$.

---

### 4.3 One-Lung Ventilation (OLV) Settings
* **FiO2:** 1.0 (100% oxygen)[cite: 4, 11].
* **Mode:** Pressure Control Ventilation (PCV) preferred to limit pressures[cite: 11].
* **Initial Tidal Volume:** 8–10 mL/kg with a 20% increase in respiratory rate[cite: 4].
* **Pressure Ceiling:** Maintain $P_{plat} < 30\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$[cite: 11].
* **Hypoxemia Interventions:** Apply CPAP to the non-dependent (operative) unventilated lung; apply PEEP to the dependent ventilated lung[cite: 11].

---

### 4.4 Anesthetic Depth & Hypothermia Targets
* **Bispectral Index (BIS) Target:** 40–60 during surgical general anesthesia[cite: 1].
* **Preoxygenation:** 3–5 minutes of normal tidal breathing of 100% O2 at high fresh gas flow, or 4 vital capacity breaths[cite: 9].
* **Minimum Fresh Gas Flow (Pediatrics):** Minimum 3 L/min to maintain inspired $CO_2 < 0.6\text{ kPa}$ (4.5 mmHg)[cite: 8].
